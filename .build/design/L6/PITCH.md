# The L6 Robot: Pitch for DeepLearning.AI

The course ends with a robot, and here's why it earns that.

Students spend four notebooks building on-device memory with their own hands: store, recall, and forget in L2; finding the right memory by meaning and by filter in L3; a full day of text, voice, and photos in L4; teaching a device to recognize objects in L5. The finale takes that exact code, the same models, the same shard, the same threshold check, and puts it behind a camera and a microphone.

On screen: the robot sees an object it has never seen and says so. I teach it with one spoken sentence: "this is my mug." Seconds later it recognizes the mug from a new angle and answers with my words, not a label. I ask "what did you see today?" and it replies from memory: the photos it saw and the things it heard, filtered to the day. Then the beat that makes the whole course land: power off, network off, power on. It still knows.

No model was retrained in those ten seconds. No language model answered that question, and no cloud was involved: retrieval alone did it, on a board that costs about as much as a textbook.

For students, this lesson is a mirror, not new material. Every stage of the robot's loop maps to a notebook they already ran, so L6 is video-only: no notebook, nothing new ships to the student repo, roughly eight minutes. The robot is their capstone, embodied.
